Common Architecture

One water. Six instructions.

Every formulation in the collection begins with mineral water drawn from high-altitude springs in the Andorran Pyrenees — zinc, selenium, copper and magnesium held in ionic balance, the cofactors the skin’s own antioxidant enzymes require in order to work.

What changes across the six is what that water carries, and when it is applied. The base is constant. The instruction is not.

02 Protocol Sequencing

The order is part of the formula.

The six are numbered because they are meant to be met in that order. Exfoliation leads: an active laid over a week of accumulated dead cells meets debris before it meets living tissue. The activation follows within minutes of it, while the surface is at its most receptive — which is the whole reason the syringe is step two and never step one.

The serum then keeps that instruction current on every day in between, and the two creams close the morning and the night: one against the environment, one against the hours when the skin does its own repair. Thinnest to richest, always. Nothing in the sequence undoes the step before it.

Used out of order, six good formulations. Used in order, a protocol.

Where the Actives Come From

Botanical signal, not synthetic mimicry.

Witch hazel, calendula, chamomile, horse chestnut, yarrow, linden, St John’s wort, mallow, peppermint. Rose and chamomile flower waters. Brown algae, red algae, watermelon extract, sweet almond, shea. The actives in this collection are drawn from plants because plant peptides speak the same chemical language the skin already uses.

Hydrolysed collagen runs through four of the six — cut short enough to be read as a signal rather than sat on the surface as a film. Every formulation is made in Barcelona, in small batches, and none of it is designed to work in a single evening.
