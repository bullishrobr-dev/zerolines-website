Day cream or night cream?

Both. They are not two strengths of one cream. Through daylight the skin is largely defending itself; overnight it moves into its own renewal, and the two formulations are written for those two states. The day cream carries no SPF and expects sun protection over it; the night cream is the last thing on the face and stays there.

Line Corrector or Refill?

The formulation inside them is the same. The Line Corrector is the precision applicator with its first cartridge already in it; the Refill is a cartridge on its own and cannot be used without the applicator. If you are starting the protocol, start with step 02. The Refill is what step 02 becomes a year later.

03 Protocol Sequencing

The order is part of the formula.

The six are numbered because they are meant to be met in that order. Exfoliation leads: an active laid over a week of accumulated dead cells meets debris before it meets living tissue. The activation follows within minutes of it, while the surface is at its most receptive — which is the whole reason the Line Corrector is step two and never step one.

The serum then keeps that instruction current on every day in between, and the two creams close the morning and the night: one against the environment, one against the hours when the skin does its own repair. Thinnest to richest, always. Nothing in the sequence undoes the step before it.

Used out of order, six good formulations. Used in order, a protocol.

04 Ingredients, So Far

What we can tell you today.

We publish a piece teaching anyone to read an ingredient list the way a formulator reads one. It would be poor manners to teach that and then hand you nothing to practise on. Here is what is settled, what is deliberately absent, and what does not exist yet.

What is named

  • Hydrolysed collagen Cut short enough to be read as a signal rather than sit on the surface as a film. It runs through four of the six.
  • Botanical peptides The half of the work you can see the same day. Named as signal peptides carried on rose and chamomile flower waters in the Line Corrector and its Refill; as a botanical peptide complex, from watermelon extract, in the serum.
  • The botanical extract complex Witch hazel, calendula, chamomile, horse chestnut, peppermint, yarrow, mallow, linden and St John’s Wort, working on the look of tone and fluid balance. In the Line Corrector and its Refill.
  • Pyrenean mineral water The base of every one of the six. Zinc, selenium, copper and magnesium in ionic balance — the cofactors the skin’s own antioxidant enzymes need in order to work.

Each formulation page names its own actives and what each one is there to do.

What is deliberately absent

  • Across all six No artificial fragrance, no drying alcohols, no sulphates, no parabens, and no silicones. No retinoids, no hydroquinone and no salicylic acid.
  • The Peeling Gel carries no abrasive particle Not one, anywhere in it. A scrub takes a little of the barrier every time, and once a week for years is a poor trade.
  • The Day Cream carries no SPF So that sun protection stays a separate, deliberate step, applied over the set cream at its own dose.
  • The Line Corrector has no needle It borrows the shape of a clinical instrument for accuracy alone. Nothing is injected, and nothing is intended to be.

What is not published yet

  • The complete list, in INCI order For any of the six. The formulations are not final, and a list published before they are would be a work of fiction we would only have to correct.
  • Everything the order would tell you Where each active falls, where the 1% line sits, and what the preservative system is. Those are the things worth reading, and we do not have them to give you yet.
  • When it arrives The full list publishes on the carton and on this site when the formulations are final and the collection goes on sale. Not before.
  • Certification as vegan Not yet held. The peptide complexes are made by fermentation, and the growth medium may involve trace dairy-derived substrates.
