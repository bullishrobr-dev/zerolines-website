# Zero Lines — the launch plan

Written 26 August 2026. A living document: amend it here rather than in chat, so the
reasoning survives.

## Three things I verified before writing this down

The research this plan came from made three load-bearing legal claims. I checked all
three against primary sources, because a launch date was going to be built on them.

**1. Gibraltar and the EU — the date was wrong, the substance matters more than the plan said.**
The treaty entered *provisional application on 15 July 2026*, not 10 April; April was an
anticipated date that moved. But the Gibraltar Government's own technical notice on goods
under the treaty says, in terms: *"After that period, they cannot be sold in Gibraltar if
they are not EU compliant."* Goods already in the market got a three-month grace period.

If that reads on cosmetics the way it reads on goods generally, then **selling a jar over
the counter at 61 Main Street requires the same EU compliance as shipping one to Madrid** —
RP, CPSR, CPNP notification. There is no soft counter-launch that skips the paperwork.

This is the single question a Gibraltar lawyer or the Office of Fair Trading should answer
in writing before anything else is planned around it. It is a phone call and it decides the
whole calendar.

**2. Omnibus VIII is real and it is unusually unforgiving.** Regulation (EU) 2026/78 applied
from 1 May 2026, banning fifteen CMR-classified ingredients and restricting hexyl salicylate
and silver — **with no transitional sell-through period at all**, which is rare. Stock that
contains a banned substance cannot be sold regardless of when it was made. This is why the
formula pre-screen in Phase 1 is the cheapest money in the plan: finding a dead ingredient in
week fourteen of a twelve-week stability run costs the entire twelve weeks again.

**3. What is not verified.** Every price, lead time and rate below came from research, not
from quotes we hold. Treat them as the shape of the thing, not as numbers to budget against,
until two RP providers and the Barcelona lab have quoted in writing.

---

## The thesis

You do not have a website problem. You have four rooms with wealthy footfall in three duty-free towns where nobody is asking anyone for anything — and since 10 April 2026 Gibraltar has sat inside a customs union with the EU, so the compliance bill you have been filing under "launching the website" is actually the price of putting your own jar on your own shelf at 61 Main Street. That inverts the whole plan: the first Zero Lines sale should be over a counter in February 2027, not online in April. No cart, no customs, no distance-selling returns law, and a customer you can watch hold the jar. Two clocks decide everything and both start in the next four weeks. The lab clock is twelve weeks of accelerated stability plus an ISO 11930 challenge test — the only item in this business that money cannot shorten, parallelise or expedite, and every week the formulas stay open moves the launch by a week, one for one. The list clock costs you roughly four contact records every trading day a counter has no tablet on it; a cruise passenger will not walk past that door again this year, and an email address is the only durable thing they can leave behind. Everything else — the cart, Instagram, the UK, paid media, SKUs four to six, a hire — waits, and the shops pay for it. Break-even on about €35–45k of cash out is roughly 385 units, or seven a week across four rooms: a number you can read off the till. The load averages 4–6 hours a week with two named spikes, and nothing in it requires you to become a content producer.

## Phase 1 — Start both clocks

**1–30 September 2026 · the heaviest month in the plan, ~8 h/week with one 10-hour week**

The twelve-week lab clock is running with samples physically in Barcelona, a Responsible Person is signed on paper, and four counters are collecting email addresses. NUMBER TO WATCH: days until the Barcelona lab confirms in writing that it has received your samples. If that confirmation does not exist by 30 September, the February counter launch is already gone and you should re-date the plan out loud rather than let it drift.

### Cut the launch range from six SKUs to three — day cream, night cream, 15ml line corrector — then commission a written pre-screen of those three formulas against Annex II and III as they stand today, naming Omnibus VIII (EU 2026/78, applied 1 May 2026 with no adaptation period) and UK SI 2026/23. Ask the assessor to also flag anything under live SCCS opinion likely to be restricted within 24 months. Leave the other three product pages live with a 2027 note and an email capture.

- **Who:** roberto decides; a paid safety assessor writes the pre-screen
- **Effort:** 2h to decide, 2h to commission. €200–500 per formula. Saves €4,000–9,000 of year-one compliance and €45,000–87,000 of first inventory versus launching six.
- **Why now:** Both halves must land before the freeze, not after. Compliance and testing scale per SKU, not per brand, and you have six lifetime website contacts — any unit split across six products is fiction. And fifteen CMR ingredients were banned outright four months ago with no grace period: discovering a dead ingredient in week fourteen costs the entire twelve weeks again. This is the cheapest insurance on the plan.

### Set a written formula-freeze date inside September and treat it as immovable. Book and pay for the lab slot BEFORE the formulas are final, so the date is owed to a third party rather than to yourself. On freeze day, samples go to Barcelona for accelerated stability (12 weeks at 40–45°C), ISO 11930 challenge testing and packaging compatibility in the actual final jar and pump — all three at once, protocol and reporting date confirmed in writing before samples ship.

- **Who:** roberto
- **Effort:** 8–10h in the freeze week, then 1h a fortnight chasing for twelve weeks. €3,000–5,700 for three SKUs.
- **Why now:** This is the entire calendar; your launch date is this date plus roughly twenty weeks. Running the three tests in parallel rather than in sequence is worth about six weeks. The jar is part of the stability result and the PAO that comes out of it is what fills the blank panel on the carton in January — so it cannot start on a provisional container, and nothing downstream starts until it ends.

### Get the Responsible Person on paper. Ask the Barcelona lab to either accept RP in writing or formally decline; in parallel get three quotes from commercial EU RP services on one identical brief — 3 SKUs, EU only, emulsions with actives. Sign a written mandate.

- **Who:** roberto; then a paid EU RP provider
- **Effort:** 4h to brief three providers and compare, then ~1h/month. €1,500–3,500/year plus €300–800 per product per year for PIF management.
- **Why now:** Under Art. 4 of Reg. 1223/2009 the EU-established manufacturer IS the Responsible Person by default, so the Barcelona lab is carrying your liability today whether it knows it or not — most solo brands find this out at the moment of notification. Until it is signed there is no PIF holder, no CPNP submission and no name and address to print on the carton, so it silently blocks artwork, print and notification all at once. Quotes take weeks to come back, which is why it starts now even though it finishes later. Paying for independence is worth it: the lab's route puts the lab's address on your carton and the lab in control of your file.

### Put a tablet on each of the four counters, locked in Guided Access to zerolines.life/analyser/?s=gib61 (and ?s=gib247, ?s=andorra, ?s=marbella). Tape one card behind each till in English and Spanish with the eight-second ask and three objection lines, offered at the dead moment — while wrapping, while the card clears, never at the door. The customer types their own email; staff never touch the keyboard. In the same briefing, state the hard rule: no samples, no testers, nothing handed over 'just to feel the texture', no deposits or reserved places, until CPNP notification is filed.

- **Who:** roberto buys and briefs; claude writes the card
- **Effort:** 4–6h one-off plus 15 min per staff member. £700–1,000 for four refurbished iPads and stands, £0/month — the existing Resend free tier covers the volume.
- **Why now:** First real move because it produces something on day one and depends on nothing else existing. At a deliberately pessimistic one reading per room per day that is ~120 names a month against a current list of one confirmed subscriber. The difference between 2% and 25% take-up is entirely the sentence used, so the card arrives with the tablet or the tablet becomes an ornament. And the no-samples rule goes in the first briefing because the instinct to hand something over arrives the moment staff start talking about a brand they are proud of — Art. 2(1)(g) makes a free sample a market placement identical to a sale.

### Thread the room into the data and fix the consent. In /Users/metabt/dev/ZLweb/analyser/index.html read `s` from location.search and post it; in /Users/metabt/dev/ZLweb/api/zero-lines-quiz-worker.js set it as the source on both the early 'analyser-started' record (line 521) and the success record. Add a second unticked box under the journal one at index.html:446 — 'Tell me when the formulations are ready to buy — one message, when there is something to say' — recorded as its own source. Add a 'Where to find us' block to buildEmail() and buildWelcome(): four addresses, the line 'nothing is for sale yet; come in and we'll go through your reading properly', and a link to /appointment.html.

- **Who:** claude
- **Effort:** 4–5h one-off, £0, none of your time.
- **Why now:** All three must land before the first name is collected, because none can be retro-fitted. Gibraltar, Andorra and Spain each require prior, purpose-specific, actively-given opt-in — wanting a weekly essay is not permission to sell to someone in February. The room tag is the only management signal you will get from four shops you are mostly not standing in. And an assessment emailed to someone standing in Marbella that never mentions the Marbella address is a lead dropped on purpose.

### Ask the three questions that cost almost nothing and decide everything downstream. (1) Phone the Gibraltar Office of Fair Trading and ask what they expect from a Gibraltar-based brand placing its own cosmetic on the Gibraltar market post-treaty. (2) Write to HM Customs Gibraltar asking, in writing, the Transaction Tax treatment of finished cosmetics imported solely for onward export and never placed on the Gibraltar market, and the procedure that secures it. (3) Sit down with a Spanish-qualified accountant and settle entity and fulfilment origin in writing — test the default: Spanish entity, RP in Spain, stock at or near the Barcelona lab, EU orders fulfilled from Spain, Gibraltar shops supplied as internal transfers.

- **Who:** roberto; the accountant is paid
- **Effort:** 4h to write and brief, then wait. €0 for both letters; €500–1,500 for the accountant.
- **Why now:** Now, before any stock moves or any account is opened, because appointments and replies take weeks and all three answers are ruinous to change later. Where the stock sits determines the RP, the Stripe entity and the cart's tax configuration — and fulfilling EU orders from inside the EU removes T2GI transit, IOSS with a mandatory intermediary and the new €3-per-HS-line flat duty in one decision. Gibraltar's Transaction Tax is 15% with no input recovery: on 3,000 units at €40 landed that is €18,000 destroyed if export stock is treated wrongly, and the published guidance genuinely does not answer it.


## Phase 2 — The twelve-week wait, spent well

**1 October – 31 December 2026 · ~3–5 h/week, one heavier fortnight for artwork**

The lab clock runs unattended while you claim the free assets that pay before any product exists and finish every launch job that does not need a lab result. This is also peak trading season, so almost nothing here is allowed to be heavy. NUMBER TO WATCH: delivered assessments per room per week in /__forms/stats. A room going flat means the ask died there, and you will not notice from another country any other way. Start at a target of three a day per room.

### Claim and video-verify all four Google Business Profiles yourself (30–120 seconds each, street sign to interior to till), then claim the same four locations on Apple Business — free since 14 April 2026 and almost unclaimed by independent retailers. List Andorra and Marbella as Natura Bella, matching the glass; check what 247 Main Street's sign actually says and list it as that. Ten real photos per room from the 296 already in /assets/img/journal, correct hours, right primary category, the WhatsApp click-to-chat link with a per-room prefill. While you are in there, configure the free WhatsApp Business app once: greeting, away message with real hours across three time zones, ten quick replies, labels.

- **Who:** roberto — video verification cannot be delegated
- **Effort:** ~90 min per room plus 2h for Apple and 90 min for WhatsApp; 10–12h total, then 15 min/week. £0.
- **Why now:** It is the only channel in this plan with a same-week revenue effect on shops that already trade, it needs no product, and GBP carries roughly a third of local-pack weight. Never run a profile whose name does not match the signage — video verification fails at the door and you can lose the profile and every review on it. A genuine rebrand later renames the same profile in place with its review history intact, which is exactly why the awkwardness is a timing problem and not a naming one.

### Claude builds two things: four thin location pages — /gibraltar-61/, /gibraltar-247/, /andorra/, /marbella/ — each with address, hours, photos, its own LocalBusiness schema copied from the only one on the site (contact/index.html), that room's WhatsApp prefill and the analyser CTA; and a three-email concern sequence after the analyser's assessment, five branches, fifteen emails, every word copied verbatim from the 57 published articles and reusing the card images already in the repo. Every channel field points at the analyser or its own location page with a tag (?src=gbp-marbella), never at the homepage.

- **Who:** claude
- **Effort:** 10–14h one-off, £0, none of your time.
- **Why now:** A profile whose website field lands on a shared contact page gives Google nothing location-specific to corroborate — today the whole 60-page site tells Google that one of your four rooms exists. And the sequence is the only 'content' that will actually happen: assembled from finished articles, it runs unattended forever and feeds the list right through November and December. Six people have ever contacted you through the homepage; the analyser is the only thing that has ever converted a stranger, so it is the destination for everything.

### Pay €1 per delivered assessment, per room, settled monthly against the per-room count in /__forms/stats, plus €20/month to the highest room. Pay on delivery, not submission, and say up front that fabricated addresses are visible and worth nothing. Keep the tally sheet visible at the till.

- **Who:** roberto; staff keep the tally
- **Effort:** 20 min/month. €120–360/month depending on volume.
- **Why now:** The wait is when a counter programme dies — without an incentive it is an ornament in about ten days, and that is its standard fate in rooms the owner is not standing in. It starts here rather than in September so the first month gives you an honest baseline to set the rate against. The competition between rooms does more work than the euro does.

### Commission the creative half of the carton only — layout, typography, colour, fixed brand copy — with the regulatory panel left as a locked, correctly-sized empty box and a deliberately generous blank area where a UK RP address block can drop in later. Get the printer's dieline before the designer starts, get quotes, and book the press slot now. EU RP address only. Print 'Made in Spain', never 'Made in EU'. Specify a genuine tamper-evident hygiene seal on every unit as part of the spec.

- **Who:** roberto briefs; a freelance designer executes
- **Effort:** 4–6h/week for three weeks, mostly approvals. €800–2,500 design; €1,500–3,500 print (quoted now, paid in January); seal €0.02–0.08/unit.
- **Why now:** This is exactly where solo launches lose a month they never budgeted. The artwork cannot be finished until the PAO exists in week twelve, and the printer then eats another 4–8 weeks — so the half that needs no lab data has to be done during the wait or February becomes April. One carton carrying the EU RP address is legal in the EU, Gibraltar and Great Britain until 31 December 2027, which saves a whole duplicate artwork and print cycle worth €1,500–4,000. And the seal is decided here or never: without a real hygiene seal the sealed-goods exception does not bite, and every opened jar of a €130 cream is legally returnable.

### Set the six prices and publish them on the existing product pages with an availability month where the buy button will go. Indicative: day and night cream 50ml €110–145, serum €130–165, peeling gel €75–95, 15ml line corrector €120–150, refill €70–85. One number per product, defended in all four rooms and online.

- **Who:** roberto decides; claude publishes
- **Effort:** 3h once. £0.
- **Why now:** A product page with no price and no button converts nobody. Publishing now makes the price do free qualifying work through the whole peak season and means you discover in October, not on launch day, whether the number frightens people. A product carrying different prices in Marbella and Gibraltar is a bell you cannot unring.

### The Instagram decision, made once in a single sitting — and this is a real fork, not a both-ways answer. Either publish 9–12 posts in one sitting (six product heroes, the Barcelona lab, the Gibraltar shopfront, pull-quotes copied character-for-character from the 44 already marked in the journal HTML), create a silent Facebook Page shell as ad plumbing, and queue 40 posts and 51 Pinterest pins in Metricool's free tier — or strip the footer Instagram link from all 86 files and keep only the sameAs entry for Google. Then put four two-hour appointments in the calendar, one per quarter, attached to the week you do quarterly accounts.

- **Who:** roberto decides and does the sitting; claude prepares the spreadsheet or strips the link
- **Effort:** 4h once for option A plus 2h per quarter thereafter; 20 min for option B. €0 either way — Metricool's free tier covers 20 posts/month.
- **Why now:** From this month the map listings start sending people there, and the site already routes visitors to an empty grid from 161 places including all six product pages — for a clinical-luxury house that is the worst impression the estate can make, and you built the road to it. Instagram's job before launch is to survive inspection, not to acquire customers, which makes it a finite task of about twelve posts rather than a habit you have said plainly you will not keep. If you will not do even the one sitting, say so and strip the link — leaving it as it is now is the only genuinely wrong answer. Captions are copied verbatim, never generated or paraphrased: with no RP appointed, an invented performance claim is an unsubstantiated claim published in three jurisdictions with a timestamp.


## Phase 3 — Results to press

**1 January – mid-February 2027 · ~4 h/week with one 10–12 hour week**

Turn two lab reports into printed cartons and a product that is legal to sell. This is the narrowest window in the plan and the only phase with a hard internal sequence. NUMBER TO WATCH: days between the stability report landing and the print file leaving your hands. Target seven or fewer — the printer's queue starts the day the file arrives, not the day the results do.

### Stability and challenge-test results land. Set the PAO, fill the regulatory panel — RP name and address, full INCI, PAO, nominal content, batch code, country of origin, GTIN — and send the file to press in the same week. Join GS1 for the barcodes if you have not already.

- **Who:** roberto + the designer + the RP
- **Effort:** 10–12h in one concentrated week. GS1 from £50/year; first print run €1,500–3,500 across three SKUs.
- **Why now:** The named spike. It is one week rather than one month only because the creative, the dieline and the press slot were all done in October. Every day it sits is a day of print lead time pushed back, and there is no way to buy that back.

### CPSR signed, PIF assembled, CPNP notification filed.

- **Who:** the appointed RP and safety assessor; roberto supplies documents and signs
- **Effort:** 2–3h of your time. €3,000–6,000 for three SKUs; the CPNP portal itself is free and takes minutes.
- **Why now:** It runs in parallel with print, not after it. Worth saying plainly: everyone treats notification as the finish line and it is one of the fastest things on the list — the twelve weeks that just ended were always the real gate.

### Place the first production order: 500 units per SKU across three SKUs, accepting the worse unit price. Split the stock at the first fill — EU-facing inventory at or near Barcelona and Marbella, Gibraltar and future UK stock in Gibraltar.

- **Who:** roberto
- **Effort:** 2–3h/week for about six weeks negotiating MOQ, spec and lead time. €27,000–33,000.
- **Why now:** Last and small, deliberately. Paying €22 a unit on 500 beats €13 on 1,500 when you cannot yet predict which of three products sells, and the standard obituary for a small skincare brand is a 70% gross margin sitting on a pallet. The split happens now because moving stock later means moving it across a customs border — and you already pay rent inside the EU customs union, so this solves the cross-border problem for free by putting boxes in the right place.

### Build one reusable product-page compliance component driven by a single data file per product — RP name, postal and electronic address, product identifiers, INCI list, warnings, batch reference — so the page, the analyser's result email and the carton copy all read from one source and cannot drift apart.

- **Who:** claude builds; roberto writes ~2h of content per product
- **Effort:** 6–10h of build, one-off. £0.
- **Why now:** GPSR Art. 19 requires all of it visible before the purchase decision on distance sales, and none of it is on the six product pages today; serious skincare buyers look for INCI before they buy. One data file because you will not hand-maintain three copies of an INCI list, and divergence between the carton and the page is itself a compliance failure.

### Commission terms, returns and withdrawal wording for both EU and UK, stating plainly that unsealed items cannot be returned for hygiene reasons and providing the model withdrawal form.

- **Who:** claude drafts; a lawyer reviews
- **Effort:** 3h once. €300–800.
- **Why now:** The sealed-goods exception is only enforceable because the seal was specified in October — the seal without the wording gets you nothing. And if withdrawal information is given incorrectly the 14-day window becomes twelve months, which on an opened €130 jar is an uncapped liability.

### Prime the list: segment the /__forms/export CSV by room and by jurisdiction, write one launch email per segment using only the people who ticked the second consent box, and hold it ready in Resend Broadcasts on the existing account and domain.

- **Who:** claude prepares; roberto writes the 200 words
- **Effort:** 2h of your time. $0 up to 1,000 contacts.
- **Why now:** The list built since September is the entire launch audience, and it must be mailed by room because Gibraltar, Andorra and Spain are three different legal sale dates. The segmentation works only because the room tag and the second checkbox went in during week one — this is the payoff for that afternoon.


## Phase 4 — Counter first

**mid-February – April 2027 · ~3 h/week**

The first Zero Lines sale happens over a counter in Gibraltar or Marbella. Same regulatory spend, weeks earlier than online, no cart, no customs, no returns law, and a customer you can watch. NUMBER TO WATCH: units per room per week, in one spreadsheet, four numbers. Seven a week across all four rooms is break-even pace on the ~€35–45k of cash out. Under seven by week eight means the counter conversation is wrong — fix the briefing, do not buy media.

### Stock lands and is batch coded. Now, and not one day before CPNP was filed, testers go on the counter and samples go in bags.

- **Who:** roberto
- **Effort:** 2h to allocate; near zero after. Sample cost is inside the first fill.
- **Why now:** This move exists to mark the date the rule flips. Before notification, free supply is 'making available on the market' under Art. 2(1)(g) exactly like a sale — a regulatory offence committed in the open in a room with your name over the door. After notification, testers are the cheapest conversion tool you own, so use them hard.

### Mail the list, by room and in legal order — Gibraltar and Marbella first, Andorra separately. No online link, because there is no cart: the call to action is the address of the room they stood in.

- **Who:** roberto sends; claude prepares the segments
- **Effort:** 2h once.
- **Why now:** This is what six months of tablet work was for, and it is worth more than the cart that follows it. Waitlist welcome emails open at over 80%, and these people handed you their address face to face in a shop rather than clicking a banner.

### Write the pricing rule down before the first sale and never break it: the price does not move, in any channel, in any of the four rooms. The launch offer is the 15ml refill included, framed as a founding-customer format — never a percentage off.

- **Who:** roberto
- **Effort:** 0 — it is a decision, not a task. The refill costs €5–6 of landed cost against €26 of contribution given away by a 20% discount.
- **Why now:** The one genuinely irreversible decision in the plan, and the pressure to break it arrives in the first eight weeks. The discount and the gift look identical to the buyer and cost five times different amounts to you — and a published launch discount permanently resets what the market thinks the product is worth. After €99, €130 becomes unchargeable, and in a clinical-luxury position the price is part of the evidence.

### Run the seated twenty-minute reading at 61 Main Street — two chairs and a table — offered only as an upgrade to people who have just done the counter reading and shown interest. Record them separately as shop-gib61-consult. No treatments, no facials, nothing performed on the skin.

- **Who:** roberto and the Gibraltar staff
- **Effort:** 20 min per booking; realistically 1–3h/week. €0–400 if the table and mirror need buying.
- **Why now:** The four-minute counter reading captures the address; the twenty-minute seated one creates the customer, and it only works now that there is something for it to end in. Nothing performed on the skin, because that is a regulated activity and a different business.

### Ask for reviews the day after purchase, by WhatsApp or email, with a plain Google review link, no incentive and no suggested wording. Reply to every review within 48 hours.

- **Who:** roberto and staff, batched
- **Effort:** 20 min/week. £0.
- **Why now:** Never at the till and never with a discount: Google's Maps policy now explicitly prohibits pressuring customers on the premises and any benefit in exchange, enforcement tightened through 2026, and Google asks reviewers directly whether they were offered something. For a brand whose whole proposition is clinical credibility, a review purge is worse than having no reviews. Your reply is the part you fully control and it is a ranking signal.

### Write '7 units a week' on the wall of the Gibraltar office and keep one spreadsheet: units per room per week. Nothing more sophisticated.

- **Who:** roberto
- **Effort:** 15 min/week.
- **Why now:** It is the only metric you will actually check, which makes it the only one worth having. It also tells you what kind of problem you have: seven a week across four rooms with this footfall is a shopkeeping number, so missing it points at the counter conversation and the staff briefing, not at a marketing budget.


## Phase 5 — Then the cart — and nothing else until a number says so

**April – June 2027, then triggers only · ~2 h/week**

Bolt online selling onto the site that already works, aim it at the one funnel with evidence behind it, keep the UK closed, then stop and wait ninety days. NUMBER TO WATCH: the 90-day repeat rate. Until it exists you cannot know what a customer is worth, and every acquisition bid and every agency proposal is unfalsifiable. Healthy is 25–30%.

### Open the Stripe account in the launch month, under whichever entity the accountant settled in September. Gibraltar and Spain are both supported; Andorra is not, so nothing in the payment stack ever routes through the Andorra entity.

- **Who:** roberto
- **Effort:** 2h once. ~1.5% + €0.25 on European cards, ~2.9% on international.
- **Why now:** Now rather than in September: an account that sits dormant for six months attracts review friction, and switching the entity behind a live Stripe account later is painful. This was the most likely hard blocker on the commerce layer and it turns out not to be one, so it needed no work until now.

### Bolt hosted checkout (Snipcart or Stripe hosted) onto the three buyable product pages with a script tag, and put the buy button inside the analyser's result email. No replatform.

- **Who:** claude builds
- **Effort:** one weekend, or 6–10h of development. $0–20/month.
- **Why now:** After the counter launch, so it is built against real till data rather than a guess about which SKU sells. The buy button in the analyser email matters more than the one on the product page: the analyser is the only asset on the property that has ever converted a stranger, and it only becomes legal to point it at a purchasable product once notification is done.

### Geo-block UK checkout, with a waitlist capture on the blocked page. Diarise 1 September 2027 to commission the GB-address carton revision, and if the UK ever opens, do it strictly in this order: appoint UK RP, file SCPN, register for UK VAT as an NETP, then switch shipping on.

- **Who:** claude builds the block; roberto diarises
- **Effort:** 2h. €0 now, and it defers €2,500–5,000 of year-one cost plus 20–30h of setup.
- **Why now:** The UK is quietly the most expensive market to open — a second RP, a second notification, VAT registration from the very first sale with no threshold at all for a non-established seller, and €3-per-HS-line duty at the border since the de minimis ended — for a market where you have no shop, no footfall and no list. The label easement runs to 31 December 2027, so the only cost of waiting is patience, and the waitlist is the instrument that later tells you whether it is worth it.

### Split online fulfilment: EU orders ship from Marbella or the Barcelona stock, UK and Gibraltar orders from Gibraltar. Add one automated reorder reminder at day 90 for the line-corrector refill — one email, not a subscription product with pausing, skipping and billing states.

- **Who:** roberto sets the allocation; claude builds the reminder
- **Effort:** 2h each, specified alongside the cart build. €0 now; €500–1,500 to retro-fit the reminder later.
- **Why now:** Routing EU parcels out of Gibraltar buys import VAT, an IOSS intermediary, flat duty and a doorstep VAT demand that turns a repeat customer into a one-off — and you already pay rent inside the customs union. The refill runs about 93% contribution on a customer already acquired, the category's real reorder interval is ~104 days, and a single reminder captures nearly all the value for none of the build or support cost. This is the mechanism that makes the numbers work without ever buying a customer — and it turns the tourist problem into an advantage: someone who bought in Marbella in July reorders from Stockholm in November.

### Then do nothing new for ninety days. Read the repeat rate, the units-per-room spreadsheet and the per-room assessment count. Keep the quarterly two-hour social batch and the 15 minutes a week on the map profiles. That is the whole job.

- **Who:** roberto
- **Effort:** ~2h/week total.
- **Why now:** This is a phase, not an omission. Every pitch that arrives in this window — agency, influencer, TikTok, Shopify — is unanswerable until there is a repeat rate, and answerable in one line once there is.

### Write the trigger list on the same wall as the units number, so the decisions are already made when the pitches arrive. Paid media: only at 500+ owned emails AND 90 days of repeat-rate data. First hire: a Spanish autónomo at €400–500/month for 6–8h/week, only once the cart generates inbound you cannot answer, inheriting the spreadsheet rather than a blank page. SKUs four to six: only after the first three sell through one full batch. Shopify: only past ~150 orders a month. Rebranding and shopfitting Andorra and Marbella: only after one room has proven it sells the product, and rename the Google profile only after the door is re-signed.

- **Who:** roberto
- **Effort:** 30 min once. €0 until a trigger fires.
- **Why now:** Each of these will be pitched to you as essential and none of them changes the outcome before its trigger. Writing them down now is what stops a good quarter's cash from becoming an agency retainer, and it converts 'am I falling behind?' into a checklist with numbers on it.

## Not doing, and why

- Replatforming to Shopify to get a cart. It is €8,000–20,000 of agency time and six months to arrive at a site no better than the one you have, and it puts 60 working pages, 57 published journal articles, 22 more scheduled to January 2027 and — worst — the analyser at risk. Three products need a buy button, not a new website: a script tag does it in a weekend for $0–20/month. Reconsider past roughly 150 orders a month, when inventory sync across four shops is a real operational problem rather than a hypothetical one.

- Paid media of any kind before launch, and probably for a year after — Meta, TikTok, non-branded Google search. Blended DTC skincare CAC averages $61 against a ~$66 AOV, so the first order is break-even at best; pre-scale brands run $35–70 CPA at 1.8–2.4x ROAS and need 8–15 fresh creative concepts a month to hold it, which you have said plainly you will not produce. Meanwhile you already pay rent on four rooms where specialty retail converts at 25–35% of people who walk in. A customer acquired at the Gibraltar counter costs a printed card; the same customer on Meta costs roughly twenty times that. Avoided: €15,000–30,000/year.

- An agency retainer (€1,500–4,000/month) or any social media hire before the cart is live and producing inbound. That is €18,000–48,000 a year to schedule posts from a spreadsheet one afternoon fills — and you would still have to brief, review and approve them, which for a one-person business usually costs more time than doing it yourself.

- Launching with six SKUs, or accepting a 1,000–1,500 unit MOQ for a better unit price. Compliance scales per SKU, not per brand, so six is €4,000–9,000 more year-one paperwork, six carton MOQs and a €72,000–120,000 inventory cheque instead of €27,000–33,000 — written against a demand forecast built on six lifetime website contacts. Serum and peeling gel keep their pages with a 2027 note and wait for twelve weeks of real till data.

- Free samples, testers, 'just to feel the texture', or gifted jars to friends of the house, in any of the four rooms, before CPNP notification is filed. Most retail advice says the opposite — testers are your launch budget — and it is wrong until February. Art. 2(1)(g) makes free supply a market placement identical to a sale, requiring the same RP, safety assessment and notification, and Spain enforces it through AEMPS with withdrawal and penalties. It also hands a regulator a documented first-placement date for a product with no file. Pre-launch, the shop's inventory is knowledge and access, not product.

- Deposits, pre-orders or 'reserve your place' payments in the rooms. In a duty-free room with wealthy visitors someone will offer. A deposit is an implicit launch date; when it slips you owe refunds in three jurisdictions with no cart and no Stripe, to exactly the people you most need on day one. Your own welcome email already says you have not set a date and would rather say so plainly — that honesty is worth more than the float. Record an intent note instead; it is worth more in February than their €200 is today.

- A launch discount, in any room or any channel, ever. 20% off a €130 cream costs €26 of contribution against €16 of landed cost, and it permanently resets the reference price. Include the 15ml refill instead: €5–6 of cost reading as €75 of value, with no published number to walk back from.

- A professional in-store skin scanner (Observ 520x, VISIA class, $20,000–$80,000 plus annual software), and any clienteling, loyalty or salon-booking platform. You already own the ritual, finished, and it runs on a £150 tablet you can carry between four rooms instead of being bolted to one. And a loyalty scheme is structurally worthless in a cruise and day-trip market — the only durable thing a tourist leaves behind is a contact record. A tablet and a CSV export is the whole system for another year.

- The WhatsApp Business Platform (API) and any BSP retainer — $20–80/month plus per-message billing, with service messages becoming billable from 1 October 2026 — to automate six lifetime inbound contacts. Equally: do not build a broadcast list on the free app. It caps at 256 and delivers only to people who saved your number, failing silently for everyone else, which would quietly corrupt every later judgement about which channel works. WhatsApp is the reply channel; email is the list. Revisit the API at ~30 inbound messages a day, or when a cart needs to send order confirmations.

- BrightLocal, Yext, Uberall, Birdeye, every review-management SaaS, and Meta Verified. All priced per location or per asset, so four rooms and three badges is $200–400/month spent monitoring rankings you have no mechanism to convert, before anything can legally be sold. Skip citation-building services entirely — for four physical shops with real signage, Google and Apple carry it. Revisit exactly one, BrightLocal at $39/month for a single location, once there is a cart and revenue to attribute.

- QR codes at the till asking for reviews, and any incentive, quota or suggested wording. Google's Maps policy now explicitly prohibits pressuring customers on the premises and any benefit in exchange, and it asks reviewers directly whether they were offered one. For a brand selling clinical credibility, a review purge is worse than no reviews. Ask the day after, by WhatsApp, with a plain link.

- Anything requiring a daily performance: TikTok, Reels, daily Stories, a Facebook content plan, LinkedIn. These are not cheap for you, they are infinitely expensive, because the currency is a habit you have said you will not keep — and short-form decays to near-zero reach in 72 hours, so a half-fed channel produces worse results than an unfed one and ends in visible abandonment. Create the Facebook Page as silent plumbing only, because it is still required to run any Meta ad including Instagram-only ones, and never post to it. Revisit video when there is a product in a box, a cart behind it, and someone paid to hold the camera.

- AI caption and content-repurposing tools (Predis, Publer AI Assist, ~$19–40/month), and any paraphrasing of your published articles. This is the one place automation is genuinely dangerous rather than merely mediocre: a generated line saying a cream 'reduces wrinkles in 14 days' is an unsubstantiated claim published permanently, with a timestamp, in three jurisdictions, by a company with no Responsible Person. Your articles are sourced and carefully hedged; a language model strips exactly the hedging that makes them safe. Verbatim copying forever — which is also what makes the job safely delegable to a €400/month freelancer later.

- Writing more journal content. 57 published, 22 more written and scheduled to January 2027, 296 finished images and 112 hand-picked pull-quotes already in the repo. The marginal article is worth approximately nothing next to the marginal till card. The bottleneck was never writing; it was cropping.

- Influencer or PR seeding. At a 500-unit MOQ the units are the scarcest asset in the business — 50 gifted units is €900–1,100 of landed cost plus roughly €6,500 of retail never taken, for unmeasurable return, at exactly the moment you need stock on four counters.

- Rebranding or shopfitting the Andorra and Marbella rooms. €15,000–40,000 a room spent before one room has proved it sells the product — and there is a sequencing cost too: Google requires the profile name to match the sign on the glass, so renaming a verified profile before the door changes risks re-verification and the loss of every accumulated review. Trade as Natura Bella, accumulate reviews under that name, rename the same profile in place at rebrand.

- Opening the UK at launch. A second RP, SCPN per SKU, UK VAT registration from the first sale with no threshold at all for a non-established seller, and duty at the border — for a market with no shop, no footfall and no list. Geo-block it with a waitlist and let the waitlist make the case in late 2027.

- A €400–1,200 written regulatory opinion on whether Gibraltar and Andorra can be sold into on a lighter basis than Spain. This was a reasonable question and one of the three research lenses recommends it — but the 10 April 2026 customs union answered most of it, and the free OFT call plus the RP conversation covers the rest. Spend that money on the Annex II/III pre-screen instead, which is the one piece of paid advice that can save you a whole quarter.

## The biggest risk

The formula-freeze date slips, and it slips quietly. It is the only genuinely serial item in this business: the twelve-week accelerated stability run and the ISO 11930 challenge test cannot be parallelised, expedited or bought down with money, and nothing downstream completes without them — no PAO, no regulatory panel, no print file, no cartons for another 4–8 weeks after that, no fill, no jar on a counter. Every other risk here has an invoice, a deadline or a person chasing it. This one has none: the Barcelona lab will not phone to ask why your samples never arrived. The failure mode is not dramatic. It is that the next six months go into the parts you understand and enjoy — the site, the images, the branding, the shop fittings — while three formulas stay 90% final, because finalising them means closing off options and every individual tweak feels free on the day you make it. All the visible work keeps producing satisfying progress (profiles claimed, list growing, cartons designed) so the slide is invisible until January, when you are standing in front of a designer who cannot finish the file and a press slot you cannot use. Freeze in September and you sell over a counter in February. Drift to December and it is May, with three more months of rent on four rooms and nothing in them. The tell is brutally simple: if samples are not physically at the Barcelona lab by 30 September 2026, February is already gone and you should re-date the whole plan out loud rather than let it drift silently. Two cheap things reduce it: book and pay for the lab slot BEFORE the formulas are final, so the date is owed to a third party rather than to yourself, and get the Annex II/III pre-screen back in writing before the freeze, so the one thing that could legitimately justify reopening a formula has already been ruled out. After that, treat any request to reopen a formula as a request to move the launch, and price it in weeks, out loud. The second-order risk, specific to the shops-first approach, is that the till ask decays to zero within a fortnight in rooms you are not standing in — but that one has a detector (the per-room count in /__forms/stats going flat) and a fix (the €1 bounty settled monthly). The freeze date has neither. It has only you.
