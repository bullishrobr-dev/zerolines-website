# Zero Lines — the launch plan

Rewritten 26 August 2026, after Roberto corrected the central assumption.

## What changed, and what it deletes

This is a **private label**. An existing manufacturer in Andorra supplies the products:
formulas, supply chain, testing, stock. Prices are known. The sequence is: trademark check
clears -> six months -> product shipped and ready.

That deletes most of what the first version of this plan was built around. Gone, and not
coming back:

- The twelve-week accelerated stability run and ISO 11930 challenge test. Theirs, already done.
- The formula-freeze date, which the old plan called the biggest risk in the business.
- Cutting six SKUs to three to save on compliance and inventory. The manufacturer carries both.
- COGS discovery, MOQ negotiation, unit-price brinkmanship.
- Commissioning a safety assessor and a CPSR from scratch.

**The critical path is no longer a laboratory. It is a trademark clearance and then a known
six-month wait.** That is a far better position: the date is knowable in advance, and the
waiting is now a runway rather than an unknown.

## The one question worth asking them

Not a blocker, and probably already covered — private-label houses do this routinely. But it
is worth one message, because it is the one duty that does not transfer with the goods.

**Andorra is not in the EU.** It has a customs union for industrial goods and is not a member
state. So a product made there and sold in Spain is an import from a third country, and
Regulation 1223/2009 requires a Responsible Person *established in an EU member state*. An
Andorran entity cannot hold that role.

Article 4(3) is the part that bites a private label specifically: whoever places a product on
the market **under their own name or trademark** becomes the Responsible Person. Buying a
finished product does not transfer that; putting Zero Lines on the carton creates it.

So, one message to the manufacturer:

> For products we sell under our own brand into the EU and the UK — do you provide the
> Responsible Person, the CPSR and the CPNP notification under our name, or do we appoint
> our own? If you provide it, which EU-established entity holds the file, and whose name and
> address is printed on the carton? And separately for the UK: who is the UK Responsible
> Person and is the SCPN notification included?

Their answer decides two things we cannot decide without it: **whose address is printed on
the carton**, and whether the UK is open at launch or later. Nothing else waits on it.

## What the six months are actually for

One thing, and it is the thing this business does not have:

**A list of people who have experienced the brand before there is anything to sell.**

Today that list is one confirmed subscriber. There are four rooms with real footfall in three
wealthy, tourist-heavy towns, and a finished analyser that runs on a tablet. At one reading
per room per day that is roughly 120 names a month — around 700 by the time stock lands.
Every trading day without a tablet on the counter is roughly four names that do not exist,
and a cruise passenger does not walk past that door twice.

That is the whole strategy for the wait. Everything below serves it.

## When the name clears — the six-month countdown

Write the ship date on the wall the day the trademark clears, and work back from it.

**Months 1-4** — the list compounds. Watch one number: assessments per room per week.
Publish prices with an availability month. Decide Instagram once, in a single sitting: either
nine to twelve posts published in one go and a monthly hour after that, or the handle stays
parked and unlinked. A dormant grid is worse than none.

**Month 5** — cartons and compliance land. The RP answer sets the printed address. Product
pages get the regulatory block: full INCI, RP name and address, PAO, nominal content, batch
code. Returns and withdrawal wording for EU and UK, reviewed by a lawyer.

**Month 6** — stock lands. Counter first: the first sale happens over a counter, not online.
Same compliance either way, weeks earlier, no cart, no customs, no distance-selling law, and
a customer you can watch. Mail the list by room, in legal order, using only the people who
ticked the buy-consent. Then testers go out — and not one day before notification is filed.

**After** — the cart. Stripe plus hosted checkout bolted onto the pages that already exist.
Then nothing new for ninety days while the repeat rate is read.

## Still not doing

- Replatforming to Shopify. Six months and thousands to arrive at a worse version of a site
  that already works.
- Paid media before launch, and probably for a year after. Blended DTC skincare acquisition
  runs near or above average order value. The rooms are the acquisition channel.
- An agency retainer or a social hire before the cart is live and producing inbound.
- A launch discount, in any room, ever. It resets the reference price permanently and this
  brand's whole position is that it does not do that.
- Free samples or testers in any room before notification is filed. A free sample is a market
  placement in law, identical to a sale.
- Deposits or reserved places. A deposit is an implicit launch date.
- A professional in-store skin scanner. The analyser already does the job on a tablet.
- Review-management SaaS, priced per location across four rooms.

## The risk now

It is no longer the formula. It is **arriving at month six with stock and no list.** The
manufacturer removed the hard part of making a product and none of the hard part of selling
one. Six months of footfall through four rooms is the only asset being built in the wait, and
it is built one counter conversation at a time or not at all.
