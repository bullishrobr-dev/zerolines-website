Editorial Standards

How the journal is written.

These nine standards are not aspirations. Each one is a decision this house has already taken, on a page you can go and read, and several of them cost us the easier sentence.

“A standard only counts on the day it costs you something.”

01 Who Is Speaking

Nobody here is invented.

Standard 01

One byline, and it belongs to the house

Every entry in the journal is signed by the Zero Lines Formulation Team, because that is who writes it. There is no rotating cast of contributors, no invented editor, and no portrait of a person who does not exist. If a piece is wrong, it is the house that is wrong, and there is nobody else for it to be attributed to.

Evidence The same byline stands at the foot of every article in the journal, without exception, from the first entry to the most recent.

Standard 02

We do not invent people

No fabricated biographies, no borrowed credentials, and no outside expert quoted to lend a paragraph an authority it has not earned. This is the standard the house has broken and repaired: a named academic at a real university once appeared in a quotation on this site and appeared nowhere else in it. The quotation came out, and the claim checker now carries a rule that flags any named outsider quoted with a title, so that a human has to confirm the permission and the affiliation before it can publish.

Where an outside voice is genuinely useful, it is cited as a paper with its authors and year, which a reader can go and check, rather than dressed up as an interview that never happened.

Evidence Every quotation in the journal is the formulation team. Outside work appears in the sources block at the foot of an article, named and dated.

Standard 03

No customer voices until there are customers

Nothing is on sale, so nobody has completed the protocol, so there is nothing a customer could honestly say about it. No purchased reviews, no composite people assembled from a brief, no five-star quotations with a first name and an initial.

Evidence The testimonials page is published and deliberately empty. Leaving it up, blank, was the harder option and the honest one.

02 What A Piece May Say

Useful whether or not you ever buy anything.

Standard 04

No figure without a source you can read

Where an article states something about how skin works, it names where that comes from — the paper, its authors, the year, and what it actually says rather than what it is usually made to say. The sources block at the foot of an article is written to be read, not to be counted.

The house publishes no figures of its own, for the reasons set out on the evidence page. That rule and this one are the same rule seen from two sides: a number is either something a reader can go and check, or it does not belong on the page.

Evidence The article on what topical skincare can and cannot do about a line made by muscle cites the prescribing information, the NHS page, and the 2000 paper behind the five-hundred-dalton rule of thumb — and then says which of its own citations does not cover chronological ageing, which is why the piece is careful to say visibly.

Standard 05

We say when the answer is a doctor

A cosmetic describes appearance. When the honest answer to a reader’s question is a clinic rather than a jar, the article says so plainly and early, rather than at the bottom in grey type.

Evidence The same article concludes that for lines produced by muscle there is no topical equivalent, and that the reader should talk to a qualified practitioner. In its own words, the reason so little useful writing exists on the subject is that the honest answer sends a share of readers to a doctor rather than to a checkout.

Standard 06

We say when the answer is not us

The journal is written to be useful whether or not you ever buy anything from this house. That means a piece is allowed to end in “keep what you have”, or “buy the cheap one”, or “this does not matter as much as you have been told”, with no product attached to the end of it.

Evidence Zero Lines does not make a cleanser, and the article on washing your face says so in order to give the advice that follows from it: keep the cleanser you have if it leaves your face comfortable, and replace it with the mildest inexpensive one at your pharmacy if it does not.

Standard 07

Interest is disclosed in the same paragraph

When an article reaches a subject the house sells into, it says so there and then — in the paragraph, not in a footer — and it states the limit of what its own formulation does. A disclosure that arrives after the argument has been won is decoration.

The house habit that governs all of this: every claim is labelled with its clock. Which effect is immediate, and which is designed to build over weeks. The industry sells the first and implies the second, and the difference between those two sentences is most of what this journal is for.

Evidence The disclosure in the muscle-lines article names the house’s own applicator, says what it does immediately, says what it is formulated to support over weeks, and states in the same breath that it does not act on muscle, because nothing available without a prescription does.

03 Dates & Corrections

Nothing is quietly changed.

Standard 08

The dates are real, and the server enforces it

The journal publishes one piece a week. There is no content system behind it — the site is hand-written files — so the schedule is enforced by the only thing that moves on its own, which is the date. Every future article ships in the build from the first day and simply refuses to exist until its turn.

Three things have to agree for that to be true rather than decorative, and all three are enforced by the server:

A future-dated article’s own address returns not-found, however you spell it
Enforced
The journal and its category pages do not list it
Enforced
The sitemap does not offer it to a crawler, so nothing indexes a page that is not there
Enforced
A link to it inside another article becomes plain text, so the sentence still reads and simply is not a link yet
Enforced

What follows from it A piece cannot be backdated to look older or more established than it is, and a piece cannot leak early to catch a news cycle. The date on an article is the date it became readable, and it is the same date the machine-readable record carries.

Standard 09

Corrections are made in the open

Articles are checked against their sources independently of whoever wrote them, and what a check finds is changed. That happens before publication where we can manage it, and the standard for what happens afterwards is this:

  • A correction is noted on the article itself, at the foot, with the date and a plain statement of what was wrong. Not a silent edit.
  • The modified date is updated in the article’s machine-readable record at the same time, so a crawler and a reader see the same history.
  • A piece is not deleted to make an error disappear. If something is wrong enough to withdraw, the page says that it was withdrawn and why.
  • A typo is a typo. Fixing spelling or a broken link is not a correction and does not get a notice; changing what a sentence claims always does.

Nothing on this site carries a correction notice today. That is a statement about how new the journal is, not a boast, and this page will be the first place it stops being true.

How to report one Write to info@zerolines.life with the article and the sentence. A reader who finds an error in this journal is doing the house a favour, and gets a reply from the people who wrote it.

04 What It Is Not For

Not a funnel.

A journal written to sell reaches the same conclusion every week. This one is allowed to reach the conclusion that you should do nothing, spend less, or ask somebody else.

  1. 01

    No manufactured urgency

    Nothing on this site is for sale, so there is no countdown, no last-chance, and no invented scarcity. When there is something to buy, that will still be true.

  2. 02

    No naming of conditions

    A cosmetics house may write about appearance. It may not offer a jar against a diagnosis, and this journal does not, however well the search traffic would do.

  3. 03

    No borrowed prestige

    No badges the house has not been given, no committee it does not sit on, and no favourable comparison with a medicine it does not make.

  4. 04

    No answer dressed as certainty

    Where the literature is thin or contested, the article says so. An article that never admits a limit is not being careful, it is being quiet about something.

Corrections

Found something we got wrong?

Send the article and the sentence. If it is a claim rather than a typo, it gets a dated note on the piece itself and a reply from whoever wrote it. That is the whole procedure, and it is short on purpose.
